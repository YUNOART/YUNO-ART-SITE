You have a logo, a design or a great idea, and you want it on a shirt, a cap or a tumbler. The next question is how: printed, embroidered, sublimated or engraved? Each method looks and lasts differently, and the right choice depends on the product, the fabric and your design.

DTF (Direct to Film)

With DTF, your design is printed on a special film, coated with an adhesive powder and then heat-pressed onto the garment. The result is a sharp, colorful print that sits on the surface of the fabric.

Why choose DTF

  • Any number of colors. Gradients, photos and fine details print as they are.
  • Any fabric color. Bright designs look just as good on a black hoodie as on a white tee.
  • Works on most fabrics: 100% cotton, polyester and blends.
  • Great for small runs. One shirt costs about the same to set up as fifty.

Keep in mind

  • You can feel the print slightly on the fabric, especially in large solid areas.
  • Like any print, it lasts longest when washed inside out and not ironed directly.

Perfect for: birthday and family reunion shirts, team shirts, events and work shirts with colorful logos. Embroidery

Embroidery stitches your design into the fabric with thread. Before stitching, the logo is digitized: converted into a stitch file that tells the machine where each stitch goes.

Why choose embroidery

  • Professional, premium look. It is the classic choice for company uniforms.
  • Very durable. Thread does not crack or peel.
  • Texture. A stitched logo looks and feels different from a print.

Keep in mind

  • Tiny text and fine lines do not stitch well. Simple, bold logos work best.
  • Gradients and photos are not possible. Each color is a separate thread.
  • Digitizing is a one-time cost per logo. After that, reorders use the same file.

Perfect for: polos and caps for a team or business that wants a stitched look.

Sublimation

Sublimation uses heat to turn special ink into a gas that bonds with a polyester coating or polyester fibers. The design becomes part of the surface instead of sitting on top of it.

Why choose sublimation

  • Vibrant, full-color results, ideal for photos.
  • Nothing to feel. The surface stays completely smooth.
  • Long-lasting on the right materials.

Keep in mind

  • It only works on polyester or polyester-coated items. It does not work on 100% cotton.
  • It needs a white or very light base. The ink is transparent, so it does not show on dark colors.

Perfect for: photo mugs and personalized gifts. See our Classic 11 oz and 15 oz mugs and the custom mugs collection.

Laser engraving

A laser removes the top coating of the material and reveals the layer underneath. The design becomes part of the object, so there is no ink that can wear off.

Why choose laser engraving

  • Permanent. It will not fade, peel or wash off.
  • Elegant. A clean, high-end look that feels like a gift.
  • Precise. Fine text and small details come out sharp.

Keep in mind

  • One tone only. The color comes from the material, not from ink.
  • It works on hard surfaces such as coated metal, not on fabric.

Perfect for: tumblers, corporate gifts and business cards that people keep. See our insulated tumbler, which offers laser engraving or full-color sublimation, and our laser-engraved metal business cards.

Which method should you choose?

  • A colorful design or a photo on a shirt or hoodie? DTF.
  • Your company logo on polos or caps for the team? Embroidery for a stitched look, or DTF for full color. (Embroidery is temporarily paused at Yuno Art, so we use DTF for now.)
  • A photo on a mug? Sublimation.
  • A gift that has to last forever, or a premium business card? Laser engraving.
  • A uniform with a logo on the chest and a big design on the back? DTF handles both. Our Premium Polo, hoodies and performance long sleeves can be printed on the chest and the back.
